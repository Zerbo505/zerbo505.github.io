## 🎼 Playing Your First Chords

Start slowly. The charm of a chord harp is in the movement, so give yourself time to feel how the keys respond. Begin with the white keys. Strum them gently and listen to how the notes flow together. That strummed sound is the heart of the Omnichord-inspired experience.

When you are comfortable with strumming, move to the black keys. These are used for picking chords. Try one chord, let it ring, then try another. Notice how changing the chord alters the color of the strummed pattern underneath.

A simple exercise is to pick a chord, strum for a few beats, then move to a nearby chord. Repeat that loop. You are already making music. As you get more comfortable, try moving between chords at different moments and see how the strummed pattern changes the feel.

Another useful exercise is to keep a steady strum going while you change chords. This helps you separate rhythm from harmony. It is the same idea behind much of what makes Omnichord-style playing enjoyable: the rhythm carries on while the harmony shifts beneath it.

## 💡 Tips for Better Results

Play with a relaxed hand. Chord harps reward light, steady movement more than force. Let the strummed notes breathe.

Practice chord changes on their own before combining them with a full pattern. Small, focused repetitions build confidence quickly.

Record a short loop if you can. Listening back helps you hear timing and chord movement more clearly than playing alone.

Keep sessions short when you are starting out. Ten or fifteen minutes of focused play is often more useful than a long, unfocused session.

Try a familiar song. Even a simple two-chord progression will help you hear how FoMni shapes harmony.

Experiment with tempo. The same chord pattern can feel calm at one speed and lively at another.
